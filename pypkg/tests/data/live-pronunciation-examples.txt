PART FOUR: LIVED AND NEARBY WORDS

In these examples, lived should rhyme with give followed by a D sound, not with loved.

He lived in a little house.
They lived here for years.
I lived near the station.
She loved the little house where he lived.
They are alive.
The lively conversation continued.
I read here every day.
We close the door.
The close race ended yesterday.
They use the screen reader.

PART FIVE: A SHORT READING PASSAGE

I live in a little house near the station. My friends live nearby. We have live music in town every Friday. Tonight we are live on air. This is a live broadcast, not a recording. My brother lived here for years, and he loved the music. We want to live here for a long time.

The reporter said that we are still live. I told her that I live nearby. We do live interviews every week. They do not live here, but they enjoy the live shows. The stream is live now, and the next broadcast starts tomorrow.

END OF TEST

The new correction handles clear contexts. It is not a complete English grammar parser. Reading the isolated word live cannot test the distinction, because it has no surrounding context.
